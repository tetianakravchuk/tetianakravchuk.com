ML capstone · model validation

Where a housing-price model breaks, and why

I compared a Random Forest against a linear baseline, then spent most of the work on the part teams skip: residual analysis, fairness across price bands, and what the model does when the input data is wrong.

Outcome: Random Forest landed at RMSE ~$290,919 and R² ~0.517 — real signal, but residuals exposed systematic error on high-end homes, so I documented it as a known production limit rather than shipping the headline number.

About

Why I moved from QA into ML

I spent a decade asking one question professionally: what happens when this is wrong? In software QA that meant broken flows and bad releases. As teams started shipping models and LLM features, I noticed the question had stopped being asked.

So I went and got the other half — an M.S. in Data Science, AI, and Machine Learning from Boston University — and the work I care about now sits exactly where the two overlap: validating training data, evaluating models and LLM outputs honestly, writing model cards someone will actually read, and catching drift before a customer does.

Reliable AI products don't start with a better algorithm. They start with trustworthy inputs, stated assumptions, and evaluation that survives contact with production.
