Problem

Publishing metadata is often fragmented across retailers, publishers, rights catalogs, libraries, and news sources. Translation credits may be missing, inconsistent, or misleading. Older and post-Soviet editions may also have indirect translation chains that are not clearly disclosed, such as a work translated through an intermediary language.

Readers, translators, publishers, and bookstores need better visibility into what exists, what is coming, and how trustworthy the metadata is.

Product idea

World Publishing Houses is designed as a shared data layer with different audience modes. Reader mode focuses on what can be read in English now, what is coming soon, and what is not yet available. Professional mode supports rights, acquisition, translation, and market-intelligence workflows.

Reader valueDiscover books by country, language, publisher, translator, availability, and reading status.
Professional valueTrack rights signals, upcoming translations, publisher activity, market gaps, and cross-country opportunities.
Trust valueShow sources, attribution confidence, translation routes, and disputed or unverified metadata.
AI / LLM evaluation

Evaluating AI-assisted ingestion before it reaches readers

The agents collect and stage publishing data; the part that matters is the evaluation gate — can the source be trusted, is the record a duplicate, is the language or rights claim safe, and should it be blocked from promotion?

  • Designed QA workflows for AI-assisted ingestion agents.
  • Evaluated source-check outputs before records could be verified or promoted.
  • Built safety gates so records stay blocked unless source confidence, duplicate checks, and review status are clean.
  • Tested LLM-style reviewer-assist flows for translation and source understanding.
  • Audited outputs for hallucination risk, unsafe claims, unverifiable rights language, and missing provenance.
  • Created production QA checks for the /wph-core/ingestion workflow.
  • Verified that AI-assisted tools never imply rights availability, market completeness, or acquisition opportunity without an official source.

LLM Evaluation

Evaluating LLM answers against the verified dataset

Because every record carries a verification status and provenance, the dataset doubles as a golden set for evaluating AI. I use it to grade LLM-generated answers about books, publishers, and translations for source-grounding and faithfulness — catching the failure modes that quietly break a source-backed product: hallucinated attributions, factual errors, ISO language-code confusion (uk means Ukrainian, not the United Kingdom), and unverified leads presented as confirmed fact.
